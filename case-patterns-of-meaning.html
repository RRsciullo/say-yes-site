Patterns of Meaning

The starting point

Start with an audit

Before there was a Patterns of Meaning collective, there was Cory Bonnet, a Pittsburgh oil painter working with salvaged and reclaimed materials. The first project was what is now the Say Yes Communications Audit: what he had, who was buying, and where to put his time. The first recommendations were to organize his contacts, start proactive outreach, and keep a press kit ready at all times. That groundwork set him up to move fast when his moment came.

That moment was a collection. Cory acquired more than 6,000 steel mill foundry patterns and their blueprints, from an era when most of these were destroyed as the mills closed. Patterns of Meaning grew out of that collection, and in time a collective of five artists formed around it. The story now had three audiences that rarely sit in the same room: the art world, manufacturing, and schools.

I can’t overstate the positive impact working with Becky has had, and is still having, on my career as a creative professional. I highly recommend sitting down with her and mapping out a plan. You’ll be amazed where you end up.

Cory Bonnet, Director, Patterns of Meaning

The strategy

Find the message

One story, said the same way every time, built on three pillars:

  • Honor the industrial heritage of the steel industry.
  • Change the idea that manufacturing careers are outdated or uncreative.
  • Inspire the next generation through the Patterns Initiative.

Every pitch, post, and report since has been built on those three lines.

The engine

Build the system

Cory and Patterns of Meaning had a wealth of press, exposure, and opportunity, but no system for sharing it consistently. The answer was a communications engine: one that turns every piece of good news into new partners, collectors, and opportunities.

The system:

  • A blog and a monthly newsletter, built and run as part of the team.
  • A steady rhythm on LinkedIn and YouTube, planned on one editorial calendar.
  • A media-ready press kit: bios, fact sheet, captioned images, and a bank of Cory's own quotes.
  • A collective CV, an impact report, a media list, and a database of school counselors across five counties.

The results

What the system made possible

So far:

  • The Patterns Initiative nearly doubled its goal. The pilot year set out to reach 1,000 students. It reached 1,858.
  • A manufacturing workforce partnership. Cory built the relationship, and he walked in with his story already organized. The partner now brings the Patterns Initiative into classrooms and tells the Patterns of Meaning story on its own.
  • Consistent press. With the story told the same way every time, the coverage keeps coming. One manufacturing writer came back for a second story on his own.
  • Museum exhibitions. Cory's work has been shown in museum exhibitions and has inspired new collaborations with other artists.

Today the work continues as ongoing, fractional communications. Cory makes the art and leads the collective. Say Yes Communications keeps the story moving.
