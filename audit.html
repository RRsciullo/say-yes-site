The audit

The Say Yes Communications Audit

Not sure where you stand? This is the fastest way to find out.

What it is

Not a technical audit. Not a brand refresh.

The audit answers one question first: does what you're already doing reach the people who want it? Then a quieter one: does all of it still say what your organization is for?

  • What's working and what isn't, across everything that's out there about you: website, newsletter, social, press, print. I do the research, so you don't have to take inventory
  • Recommendations in priority order, written so anyone can act on them

Patterns of Meaning started with one. Read the case study →

When it helps most

Any time you want a clear picture.

The audit works whenever you want to know where you stand. It earns its keep fastest when something is changing: a new executive director, a new plan, or a new communications hire.

A new hire, for example, gets a documented starting point: what already exists, what's working, where the gaps are, and what to tackle first. They start with shared priorities instead of spending their first months piecing together the past.

What you get

A report you can act on, whether or not you hire me next.

Written report.

Everything that's out there, reviewed and laid out plainly, with recommendations in priority order, so it reads clearly to your board, your staff, or a new hire.

Live walkthrough.

An hour where we go through it together, not a document dropped in your inbox.

A follow-up plan.

Questions

What people usually ask.

Do I have to hire you for more after this?

No. You get a report and a plan either way.

What if we're not sure we're ready for ongoing work?

That's exactly what the audit is for.

How much of our time does it take?

A short conversation at the start, and the hour-long walkthrough at the end. I do the research and the rest.

Do you work with societies and volunteer-led groups?

Yes, along with arts organizations, nonprofits, and small businesses. For a member-based group, the audit also looks at how communications move between the board, committees, members, and the public, so the result is a system volunteers can keep up with.

Is this a marketing audit or a brand audit?

Neither. It's a communications audit: what you're saying, where, and whether it's reaching who you built it for.

Ready to see where you stand?

Tell me what you're working on and who you're trying to reach. If I'm not the right fit, I'll say so.
